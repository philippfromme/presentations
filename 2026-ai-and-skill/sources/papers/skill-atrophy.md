# Skill Atrophy (assembled notes)

No single dedicated paper; this collects the most relevant findings on skill
decay / deskilling under AI and automation.

## Lee et al., CHI 2025 — GenAI and Critical Thinking
Survey of 319 knowledge workers, 936 first-hand GenAI work examples.
- Higher confidence **in GenAI** → less critical thinking enacted and less effort.
- Higher **self-confidence** → more critical thinking.
- GenAI shifts critical thinking toward information verification, response
  integration, and task stewardship rather than original generation.
- DOI: 10.1145/3706598.3713778

## Bainbridge 1983 — Ironies of Automation
- Automating a task removes the operator's routine practice, yet the human is
  still expected to take over in exactly the difficult, non-routine cases the
  automation cannot handle.
- The more reliable the automation, the more the residual human skill decays —
  and the harder manual takeover becomes.

## Automation complacency / automation bias
- Automation complacency: "poorer detection of system malfunctions under
  automation compared with under manual control" (NASA ASRS / Parasuraman).
- High perceived reliability → operators disengage from monitoring → reduced
  situational awareness and slower re-assumption of control (Endsley 2017).
- Complacency is sharply reduced when reliability *varies* over time, but is
  NOT reduced by experience/practice and is hard to train away.
- Related: automation bias (over-trust in automated suggestions), errors of
  omission and commission, "learned carelessness".

## Skill retention / non-persistence (via Shen & Tamkin)
- Wu et al.: GenAI improved immediate performance on content tasks (Facebook
  post, performance review, welcome email), but the gain did NOT persist on
  subsequent independent tasks.
- Wiles et al.: AI as an "exoskeleton" for non-technical consultants — enhanced
  technical ability did not persist once AI access was removed.

## Who is most exposed
- Less-experienced / lower-skilled workers get the largest productivity uplift
  from AI (call center, consulting, legal QA, writing, software). They are also
  the group still meant to be acquiring skills — the most to lose to atrophy.

## 5 Facts (as used in DRAFT.md)
1. Higher trust in AI → less critical thinking (Lee et al.).
2. "Ironies of automation": skill still needed decays as automation grows (Bainbridge).
3. Automation complacency: monitoring skill decays with reliable tools; not trainable away.
4. AI skill gains don't persist once removed ("exoskeleton"; Wu/Wiles).
5. Novices are most exposed.
